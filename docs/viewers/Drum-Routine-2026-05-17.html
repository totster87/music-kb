1 — ★ Morgenstein: Drum Set Warm-Ups (first session) 10 min
Full kit warm-up · pp.5–7 · No tempo target · Find the day's tempo
  • Open the score — first 2 min: scan past any intro/foreword and find the first real exercises
  • Pick whichever pattern looks accessible — single strokes, 8th-note coordination, simple kit warm-up
  • No click for the first 5 min — let the body wake up at the tempo it wants
  • Then click on at 60 BPM and play one pattern through cleanly 4×
  • Goal isn't to push — it's to find a tempo where everything (hi-hat, snare, bass, hat foot) lands together

3 — PI Rock: Section 3 — Right Hand Up-Beats 10 min
Progressive Independence Rock · Spagnardi · pp.57–59 · New section
  • Right hand stays on up-beats (the &) — strong contrast to S1 (8ths) and S2 (quarters)
  • Snare + bass independence underneath — read the first 2–3 exercises
  • Start at 70 BPM — slower than S2 because the up-beat feel is destabilising
  • If the right-hand up-beat slips back onto down-beats when bass+snare get busy, drop tempo
  • One full clean exercise > four sloppy ones
Fresh section: S1 + S2 have been worked recently. S3 (Right Hand Up-Beats) is genuinely new territory — the right hand anchor is on the off-beats instead of the on-beats. Expect the body to want to flip it.

4 — Donati: Bass Drum Flams (Chapter 9) 10 min
Double Bass Drum Freedom · Donati · pp.109–111 · New chapter
  • First time touching this chapter — bass drum flams = both feet hit closely staggered, not unison
  • Read p.109 first, identify the sticking notation for feet
  • Start hands-off at 60 BPM — just feet, get the flam tight
  • Add hands (ride + 2&4 snare) on top once feet are steady
  • If feet revert to unison hits, drop tempo and isolate
New chapter: Different from sextuplets (Ch.5, 80 BPM ceiling), paradiddles (Ch.8 worked 2026-05-15), hand-foot independence (Ch.2 p.21). Flams are a coordination quirk — a hair of stagger between feet rather than tight unison.

5 — ★ Jungle / Drum 'n' Bass (first session) 15 min
Sight-read pp.40–42 · No tempo target · Find one groove to claim
  • First 4 min — scan p.40 to p.42: read the patterns visually, identify the kick/snare placement of an Amen-style break vs. a more syncopated DnB groove
  • Next 6 min — pick ONE groove pattern and play it slow (start 75 BPM). DnB lives at 160+ BPM — but learning at half tempo first
  • Next 3 min — push it: double the click to 150 BPM if half-tempo felt the same. See where the pocket breaks.
  • Last 2 min — log: which page, which pattern, what felt foreign vs natural, the BPM where it broke
  • This is a different vocabulary from metal — ghost notes on the hi-hat and offbeat snares are the texture
★ First session on this book: Brand new vocabulary — high-tempo breakbeat grooves. Goal is reconnaissance, not mastery. Update library/jungle-drum-n-bass.md after the session with the page/pattern you claimed.

7 — Burnout: Sextuplet Sprint 5 min
Rotation from kick+snare unison · No click · Push every minute
  • Groups of 6 on the feet — RLRLRL alternating
  • Simple hands on top (ride + 2&4 snare) so feet are the focus
  • Start at a tempo you can hold — push every 60 seconds
  • Stop when the sextuplets blur into 5s or 7s — that's the actual ceiling for now
  • No looking at a click — feel the grouping
Rotation: Last burnout was Kick+Snare Unison (2026-05-15). Before that Straight 16ths (2026-05-06). Today: feet-only sextuplets. Different muscle.
