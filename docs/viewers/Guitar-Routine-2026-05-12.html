Warm-Up
01Upstroke Accent Drill 7 min
Alt picking · 16th notes · No click then click · 60–90 BPM
  • 16th note alternate picking on a single string — accent the 1, play 4 bars
  • Shift the accent to the e — 4 bars. Then &, then a
  • Each accent position: the accented note should be noticeably louder, upstroke or not
  • When the upstroke accent feels equal to the downstroke accent, move to the next string
  • Keep the unaccented notes quiet — the contrast is the exercise
Why this drill: Last session — upstroke notes uneven in volume, especially the first upstroke in each group. This exercise forces the upstroke to carry full weight on demand. Fix the mechanics before playing repertoire.

DEP — Cold Read
04DEP — Prancer, pp.10–11 10 min
Dillinger Escape Plan · One Of Us Is The Killer · Standard · Cold read
  • No prep — cold read straight off the page, whatever tempo makes it feel like music
  • DEP writing is rhythmically complex — read the bar lengths before you play a note
  • Don’t try to sound like the record on the first pass. Mechanics first, feel second
  • If a bar is genuinely unreadable: isolate it, figure out the subdivision, then move on
Goal: Cover both pages at least once. Doesn’t have to be clean — the point is to encounter new material and start building the vocabulary.

ABR Treatment — Pinch Harmonic Isolation
05Treatment — PH on G String 8 min
ABR · Treatment · Drop C · No click · Isolated
  • Focus on G string only — don’t attempt D or A string until G is consistent
  • Experiment with pick angle and contact point — note what fires and what doesn’t
  • Try: slightly more pick exposed, squeeze thumb immediately after pick contact
  • Hit rate target: better than 50%. Count your attempts — track it
  • Once a position reliably fires, do it 10× in a row before calling it a win
From last session: G string hit rate ~50% or less. This block is about finding the exact pick contact geometry that fires reliably — not about playing in context yet.
