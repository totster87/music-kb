Warm-Up
01Stick Control p.20 — Flam Patterns 7 min
George Lawrence Stone · Stick Control · p.20 · 50–75 BPM
  • Flams chapter — new territory, not the single-beat or roll combos from previous sessions
  • The flam grace note must be quiet and nearly simultaneous — not a separate stroke
  • Both directions: flam, flam accent, and flam tap. Don't rush to the next pattern
  • On pad first; when consistent, move to kit and let the flams accent tom transitions

02Latham — Advanced Funk Studies p.26 8 min
Rick Latham · Advanced Funk Studies · p.26 · Listen first
  • Transcriptions chapter — Gadd, Garibaldi, Mason, Erskine. This is the deep end of the book
  • Different format to the exercises: these are transcribed real grooves, not drills
  • Read the whole page before touching the kit — find the shape of the groove first
  • Play at whatever tempo makes the groove feel like music, not like an exercise
Approach shift: Exercises on p.20/24 were about building patterns. This page is about copying a real feel — listen to the feel of each groove before playing it.

Independence
03PI Rock p.70 — Section 3, Up-Beat Ride 8 min
Ron Spagnardi · Progressive Independence Rock · p.70 · 70–95 BPM
  • Section 3 — ride hand on up-beats only. Completely different feel from eighth notes (S1) and quarter notes (S2)
  • The up-beat ride wants to feel like jazz but the patterns underneath are rock — sit with that tension
  • Don't let the ride hand drag. Up-beats must land exactly halfway between the click pulses
New section: p.9 was S1, p.45 was S2. This is S3 — the material gets progressively more demanding with each section.
Progressive Independence Rock, p.70
Jazz / Bop
04Riley — Art of Bop Drumming p.36 8 min
John Riley · Art of Bop Drumming · p.36 · Slow–medium
  • Soloing section — past the Time Playing and Comping chapters worked recently
  • Riley solos are built on vocabulary, not flash — read for the logic before playing for the feel
  • Keep the ride going. The solo doesn't stop the time — it grows out of it
  • One chorus at a time, slow. Don't skip to the next pattern until this one feels like a statement
Art of Bop Drumming, p.36
Double Bass
05Donati — Double Bass Freedom p.65 8 min
Virgil Donati · Double Bass Freedom · p.65 · 60–85 BPM
  • 16th Note Triplet Series — a new chapter. Sextuplet groupings, alternating feet
  • Different from p.59 (which was also sextuplets but an earlier exercise set in the same chapter)
  • The triplet feel on the feet is harder to control than straight 16ths — let it breathe
  • Add a simple hand pattern only once the feet are solid — don't multi-task too early
Reference: 05/05 session did p.59 at 60–80 BPM. p.65 is deeper in the same chapter — expect similar difficulty, possibly harder groupings.
Double Bass Drum Freedom, p.65
Coordination
06Chapin — Advanced Techniques p.32 7 min
Jim Chapin · Advanced Techniques Vol.1 · p.32 · Slow–medium
  • Sixteenths chapter — different subdivision to p.18 (dotted eighths) and p.25 (triplets) from recent sessions
  • Right hand plays continuous 16ths on ride; independence underneath in left hand, bass drum, hat foot
  • If coordination breaks: stop, isolate the two limbs causing the conflict, rebuild from there
  • Metronome on 2+4 — makes the independence feel musical rather than mechanical

Burnout Finisher
08Open Sprint 3–5 min
No click · No rules · Full kit · Maximum
  • No structure. No tempo. No goals. Just play as hard and fast as possible
  • Full kit — use everything. Don't stay on one sound
  • 3 minutes minimum. 5 minutes if you have it
  • When you think you're done, play 30 more seconds
Rotation: Straight 16ths (05/06) → Sextuplet sprint (05/09) → Kick+Snare Unison (05/10 morning). Open sprint today — no rules, no form, full expression.
