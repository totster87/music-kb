Drum Routine

Sunday 3 May 2026  ·  ~55 min
Warm-up 10 min Independence 15 min Double bass 15 min Table of Time 10 min Cool-down 5 min
1 — Warm-Up: Stick Control 10 min
Stick Control · Stone · p.5
  • Single Beat Combinations — rows 1–8 on page 5
  • Practice pad or snare — both hands even
  • Start at 60 BPM, 16th notes
  • Accent the first note of each group — feel the pattern, don't just execute it
  • Push to 80 BPM once 60 feels loose and relaxed
  • No tension in the wrists — this is a warm-up, not a test
Stick Control — p.5
2 — Independence: PI Rock Part 3 15 min
Progressive Independence: Rock · Spagnardi · p.9
  • Exercises 1–4 only — don't jump ahead
  • Hi-hat foot on beats 2 and 4 throughout
  • Start slow enough that hands and feet are truly independent — 60 BPM
  • If a foot pattern causes the hands to hesitate, that's the rep to repeat
  • Goal: each exercise clean × 4 bars before moving to the next
  • Don't rush to exercise 4 — depth over distance
Key principle: The hands should not know what the feet are doing. If you're thinking about the kick pattern while playing the snare pattern, slow down until the feet become automatic.
PI Rock — Part 3, p.9
3 — Double Bass: Morello Groups of Four 15 min
Master Studies · Morello · p.9 · Last session: 70 BPM
  • Baseline established at 70 BPM last session — start there again to confirm
  • Once 70 feels solid, try 72 BPM — only move up if the pattern is clean and relaxed
  • Focus on even stroke weight between left and right foot — the weaker foot tends to ghost
  • Keep the upper body (hands) steady while the feet work — don't let the kick pattern affect the groove
  • If 70 feels rough today, stay there — don't force the tempo up
Watch for: Left foot dying off at the end of each group — fatigue sets in fast when double bass is new. Short reps with full recovery beat long reps with sloppy feet.
Master Studies — Groups of Four, p.9
4 — Table of Time 10 min
Master Studies · Morello · p.8 · Last: 53 BPM halftime
  • Start at 53 BPM halftime — same as last session baseline
  • Work through subdivision transitions slowly — this is where rushing happens
  • At each subdivision change: stop, breathe, feel the new value before playing it
  • The point isn't speed — it's hearing each subdivision clearly before moving to the next
  • If a transition rushes, isolate just that pair of subdivisions and loop the crossover
Known issue: Rushing on subdivision transitions. Fix: count the new subdivision out loud for one bar before playing it. The brain anticipates before the body catches up — slow the anticipation down.
Master Studies — Table of Time, p.8
5 — Cool-Down 5 min
Free play
  • Free groove — no structure, no click
  • Easy tempo, light touch
  • Let the hands and feet decompress
After session: Note max comfortable BPM on Groups of Four, whether Table of Time subdivision transitions felt cleaner, and how the PI Rock independence exercises felt. Log it.
