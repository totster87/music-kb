1 — Warm-Up: Reed Syncopation Reading 10 min
Progressive Steps to Syncopation · Reed · p.37
  • Read the line as written on snare — no interpretation yet, just accurate reading
  • Start at 60 BPM, 8th note pulse — feel the syncopation, don't anticipate it
  • Once the line is under control: orchestrate it around the kit, keeping the pulse on kick and hi-hat
  • Push to 80 BPM once the reading feels relaxed
Key principle: Hear the rest as long as the note before it. Rushing at syncopated rests is the most common reading problem.
Progressive Steps to Syncopation — p.37
2 — 30 Dirty Grooves 10 min
30 Dirty Grooves · p.8
  • Read through the page before playing — get the shape of the groove in your head first
  • No click to start — feel the pocket before locking to a tempo
  • Add click once the pattern is under fingers — lock in at whatever tempo feels natural
  • Push one groove to the point where it breaks, then back off and hold the edge
Mindset: Dirty grooves are supposed to feel like they're about to fall apart. If it feels completely safe, it's too slow.
30 Dirty Grooves — p.8
3 — Donati: Hand-Foot Independence 15 min
Double Bass Drum Freedom · Donati · p.21 · First session on this section
  • Read through the page before playing — understand how the hands and feet interact
  • Start at 55 BPM — new material, go slower than feels necessary
  • Hands and feet truly independent — if the kick disrupts the hands or vice versa, drop the tempo
  • Short bursts (4–8 bars), full rest between — don't grind through coordination breakdowns
  • This is different from the sextuplet work — it's about coordinating patterns, not pure speed
First session: Get a feel for the Donati hand-foot system. Even one exercise clean at slow tempo is a win.
Double Bass Drum Freedom — p.21
4 — ABR Messengers: Truth of a Liar 15 min
After the Burial — Messengers · Drum Transcription · Song 1 · pp.7–17
  • Read through the transcription first — identify the sections before playing anything
  • Pick one section and work it out slowly — don't try to run the whole song
  • No click — play along to the record to lock into the original feel and tempo
  • Note which sections are approachable now and which need more technique work first
Context: You're already learning this on guitar — hearing the drum part gives you the full picture of how the two parts lock together.

5 — Burnout: Straight 16th Endurance 5 min
No click · No structure · Push until form breaks
  • Both feet alternating 16ths — find your max comfortable tempo and sit just above it
  • Simple hand groove on top — ride + snare on 2 and 4, don't drop the upper body
  • Hold until the feet start to blur, then rest 30 seconds and go again
  • 2–3 rounds max — this is a sprint, not a grind
